# Economy and construction

The current simulation defines **107 goods, 146 gear items, 40 processing recipes and 90 structure kinds**. These are simulation definitions; the larger artwork catalogue includes additional visual concepts.

## Goods and equipment

Goods have quantities, food value, bulk and spoilage. Bands share stores and people carry packs. Food is consumed in perishability order; heat accelerates spoilage while storage and preservation reduce it. Gathering and processing produce named resources rather than an unlimited generic material pool.

Tools improve the relevant jobs, clothing contributes warmth, armour reduces damage, weapons affect hunting and combat, and decorative gear contributes prestige. Equipment wears out. Crafters use available resources and workstations to make missing supplies and equipment.

Recipes cover food preservation, milling and baking, dairy, brewing, textiles, leather, charcoal, pottery, bricks, masonry, smelting, glass, concrete and advanced components. Technology and workstation requirements gate production.

## Buildings and work

Bands choose projects according to need, available knowledge and materials. They site footprints, clear land, obtain inputs and add labour. Buildings can be planned, under construction, complete, damaged, ruined or burned. Structures age, are repaired, can be abandoned, and may later be adopted. Portable shelters move with a migrating band.

Dwellings, storage, hearths, workstations, fields, orchards, pens, wells, shrines, palisades, vessels and vehicles are represented. Farms track crop growth and harvest; herds breed and provide produce. Villages can expand and send pioneers elsewhere.

Regional building styles choose walls, floors and roofs for the biome. Construction still consumes the resources declared by the structure definition; a regional visual material does not automatically introduce a separate physical recipe for every wall variation.

## Storage and transport limits

Inventories currently hold quantities by good type. Container sprites show representative stored contents. They are **not** independently authoritative inventories with persistent stack identities for every chest, granary, cart and backpack.

Sea travel supports remembered shores, technology-dependent ranges, migration and losses at sea. Transport art includes vessels and land vehicles, but vehicle cargo scheduling, loading reservations, per-container theft and individual stack transport are incomplete. Asset metadata and conservation examples describe the intended integration contract; they do not turn those examples into gameplay.
