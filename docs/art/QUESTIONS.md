# Art specification decisions

## Foundation — 2026-10-30

- The required skin, hair, cloth and culture ramps alone require 156 shade entries. Preserve all required ramps and exact UI anchors; treat the approximate 96–128 master colour target as advisory. Runtime slots remain below 256. The master includes the union of all LUT colours.
- Use four seasons for all biomes except savanna, tropical seasonal forest and tropical rainforest, which use wet/dry. Wetland uses four seasons because its ID does not establish a tropical climate. Water biomes use four seasons. Explicit row labels are stored in palette.json; no simulation IDs change.
- LUT rows are complete 256-pixel tables. Defined slots are opaque; unused slots are transparent. Fixed material slots remain identical in every row. Character LUTs change only their own channels; compose nature, skin, hair, cloth and culture rows by slot name, rather than replacing the entire lookup table with each selection.
- The reference uses temperate forest summer, skin tone 3, brown hair 2, undyed cloth 12 and culture hue 0. A single cloth LUT sets both cloth channels; independent clothing colours can be composed by slot range.
- Foundation contains no sprites, animations, corner sets or glyphs. Animation, seam, silhouette, paired index-map and banned-glyph validation must be implemented with those phases; foundation validation does not claim those checks are complete.

## Terrain — 2026-10-30

- Section 3.1 is the next phase after the foundation. Overlays in section 3.2 are a separate phase and are not generated here.
- The priority list omits `path`. Place it immediately above `dirt` and below `tilled_soil`; all explicitly ordered material pairs retain their relative order.
- Plain and corner sets are separate sheets under each unchanged material ID: `terrain.<material>_plain` and `terrain.<material>_corners`, with `material` holding the simulation ID. Horizontal columns are animation frames; vertical `sheet_rows` are variants or masks, not facing directions. Static terrain uses `directions: ["none"]`. Mask zero is intentionally transparent.
- Tilled soil gets four plain variants and a full corner set for each of N–S and E–W furrows, in that order. Its sidecar lists both orientations and the row layout.
- All terrain exports include index maps, including fixed material slots. Dry grass uses the straw-coloured thatch ramp rather than summer-green grass; forest floors combine foliage and soil. Future shaders may remap fixed slots explicitly for additional climate effects.
- Water foam occupies the outer contour on the land side of the underlying 0.5 coverage threshold. It shares water's lightest slot. Masks use a deterministic wobbled dual-grid field and agree exactly along compatible edges.
- Cliff assets are rock/scree surface faces without ground shadows. Their corner masks use the same dual-grid convention, with separate south wall strips. The engine supplies height placement and shadows.

## Overlays — 2026-10-30

- Section 3.2 is generated in this phase, including the optional footprint trails. Sizes not specified for rivers, roads, borders, seasonal cover and surface effects use the 16 px base tile. Cracks have four variants at each of 16 and 32 px; craters have one each at 32 and 48 px.
- River and road connection bits are N=1, E=2, S=4, W=8. Mask zero is an isolated pool or road patch. River widths are 4, 8 and 12 px including the bank highlight; road widths are 4, 6 and 8 px. Animated overlays run at 4 fps, since the specification supplies frame counts but no timing.
- `asset_id` records the unchanged specification ID. Subtype and size suffixes distinguish sheets, e.g. `overlays.river_stream` and `overlays.crack_32`. Columns hold animation frames, rows hold masks or variants, following the terrain layout convention.
- Territory borders are single-pixel dashed contours, not filled territory masks. Masks 0 and 15 are empty; complementary masks may share identical contour pixels because they describe opposite sides of the same boundary. They are masks, not artistic variants. Ambiguous diagonal masks trace two disconnected corner boundaries. Transport junctions are the required network topology, not culture symbols.
- Append `culture_white` at slot 156 without shifting any existing slot. Its reference is the palette's bone white, and culture LUT rows map it to their lightest hue. Other LUTs preserve its pale reference; compose the culture selection by slot name alongside the other culture slots. No additional master colours are needed.
- Full-tile surface overlays use hard-edged sparse patches within a 16 px footprint so the underlying land remains visible. Their transparent edges match across all three variants. Wet ground and burned ground use material marks, not cast shadows. Flood water has four frames per variant.
- Decal pivots use the bottom-centre of their footprint. Crater relief is surface shading under top-left light; there is no cast ground shadow.

## Flora — 2026-10-30

- Section 4 is generated as 23 plant IDs across 72 state/size sheets. State and size suffixes identify sheets while `asset_id` keeps each specification ID. Rows are shape variants, columns are time samples; vegetation has no directional facing.
- Use 16x16 for both conifer and broadleaf saplings, 16x32 for broadleaf young/mature/bare/dead/burned, and 32x32 for old broadleaf. Add a matching 32x32 `broadleaf_old_bare` sheet for winter on old trees; it is the old growth stage with the bare seasonal state. Both cactus sizes receive all three variants in normal and flowering states.
- Trees use the specification's shader sway option, recorded with a one-pixel maximum displacement and a base anchor. No sway shader is implemented in this asset phase. Tall grass and reeds have four distinct exported frames at 4 fps, with a stationary base.
- `seasonal_states` maps broadleaf young/mature to the matching-size winter bare sheet, old broadleaf to old bare, and acacia to its dry bare sheet. Seasonal contact rows select that silhouette as well as the LUT. Sapling leaf removal and additional age-specific bare shapes remain a runtime foliage-mask option; the specification lists one bare state per species, not a full age-by-season product.
- Conifer snow caps are transparent extra layers matching mature variant indices and pivots. `composite_over` names the matching mature sheet; apply the same sway displacement to both. The contact sheet shows the composed result. Other snow accumulation amounts and tree ages can use the seasonal overlay or runtime masking; the authored cap is the mature template.
- All pivots are at `[width / 2, height - 2]`, leaving a transparent pixel below the footprint. Ground shadows are supplied by the engine, with the size class recorded in each sidecar.
- Append four `fruit_0..3` slots after the existing registry, starting at 157. Their reference colours use the red cloth ramp; cloth LUT selections recolour them independently of foliage. Existing slot indices and master colours remain unchanged. Flowers and mushroom caps use the existing flower slots.
- Charred tissue uses fixed basalt ramp slots so seasonal lookup changes cannot turn burned plants green. Dry and withered tissue uses the fixed straw ramp, and cut wood interiors use fixed wood. Live leaves, bark, stems, blooms and fruit retain their respective variable slots; every sheet includes its index map.

## Fauna — 2026-10-30

- Section 5 includes 17 wild/ambient entries and six domestic forms: fowl, goat, sheep, horse, cattle and dog. Cattle inherits the bison frame size and animation list, with its hump removed; dog inherits the wolf list, with folded ears and domestic colouring. Both keep their explicitly named IDs. Other domestic sheets retain the original `asset_id` and set `form` to `domestic`.
- Each animation is a separate constant-size horizontal strip. IDs use `fauna.<animal>_<animation>`; domestic forms of otherwise identically named animals use a `_domestic` qualifier. Directions are `["side"]`, facing right with `mirror_left: true`. Rows hold coat patterns, not directions: one wild reference, or three domestic patterns. `sheet_rows` describes the layout and `anims` supplies the timing for a selected row.
- Frame counts and sizes follow the section 5 table. FPS is not specified there, so use idle/howl/stand 3, walk/fly/flap 8, run 12, eat 4, swim/crawl/slither 6 and attack/strike 10. Dead frames have FPS 0. Snake has no dead state because none is listed.
- Domestic base coats use the existing four `hair` slots and all eight rows of `lut_hair`; light patches use the fixed hide ramp. This provides solid, front-patched and rear-patched coats without adding palette colours or slots. The seed adjusts the patch boundary. Wild bodies use fixed hide or darker basalt, with wood-coloured horns/antlers and bone-white teeth.
- Ground-animal pivots are `[width / 2, height - 2]`, with a transparent pixel below their standing foot baseline. Frames may lift feet during a run. Bird flocks and fish schools have no feet and instead use a centre pivot. Each ambient sprite contains four animals. No cast shadows are baked into any frame.
- Death is a flattened resting silhouette with closed eyes, without blood or gore. Predators use darker bodies and visible fang accents; their attack strips show a jaw/neck motion rather than an impact effect. Combat effects remain in their own later phase.

## People sprites — 2026-10-30

- Sections 6.1–6.2 form this phase. The 48x48 portrait system in section 6.3 is a separate following phase. Export 140 sheets: the seven ordered layers for each of the twenty animation IDs, including separate eat and drink strips. Simulation action IDs retain the specification's spelling and mapping.
- Each sheet has 16x16 frames across time. Select a logical part variant and an age/build/direction profile, then use `row_lookup[variant_index][profile_index]` to find its physical row. Identical rows share storage, including occluded paint and direction-invariant hair. `sheet_rows` labels the physical rows; `profiles`, `variants` and `row_lookup` preserve every logical choice. This avoids shipping duplicate pixel data without inventing visual differences for hidden parts.
- Standing reference body heights are child 8, adult 11 and elder 10 px. Torso widths are 2, 3 and 4 px for slim, average and stout. Pose-specific limb reaches, raised hands and hair can extend beyond those reference body heights. Elders lean forward in side view. Sleep and dead use lying profiles, craft uses seated side profiles, mourn is kneeling, swim contains only head/shoulders, and play has child profiles only.
- `people/rig.json` holds the pose geometry, exact layer order, ten hairstyle definitions and action mappings. Shaved selects no front/back hair layers; other styles select their matching front layer and optional back layer. No duplicate transparent shaved images are exported. Default elder hair selects grey LUT row 6; white remains available through row 7.
- The layer order puts paint after clothing, while its description requires it under clothing edges. Preserve that exact order and clip paint with `body_alpha & ~clothing_alpha` before compositing. `clip_rule` records this requirement; the preview assembler implements it. Paint that has no exposed skin in a pose is intentionally transparent. No renderer or simulation changes are made in this phase.
- There are six clothing types, six invented paint patterns and the five accessory types. Helmet has leather and bone material variants (`helmet` and `helmet_bone`). Clothing uses cloth slots with a culture accent for ceremonial trim; there is no fixed religious glyph. Held items retain all ten specified IDs, including the carried infant.
- Hand anchors and pivots are indexed by logical profile, independently of shared physical image rows. Each `hand_anchors[profile_index][frame_index]` is the grip pixel for every held item variant. Held items turn according to hand height to fit the 16x16 frame. Mirroring side-facing sprites also mirrors anchors and pivots with `x -> 15 - x`.
- Grounded poses use `[8,14]`; lying poses have age-specific foot-centred pivots in `row_pivots`. Use the profile's pivot instead of the sheet's fallback pivot when they differ. Torso lift and head bow supply breathing/prayer/mourning motion even when hands are hidden by clothing.
- Each part and the assembled reference animation figures are checked against a sixteen-colour ceiling. Palette colours are never interpolated in the exported PNGs. Runtime recolouring and composition must follow the metadata; only the preview assembler is implemented here.

## Portraits — 2026-10-30

- Section 6.3 is generated as fourteen static part atlases. The thirteen declared layers retain their exact order; the hair layer contains back and front sublayers. Every tile is 48x48, head-and-shoulders, three-quarter facing left. Child, adult and elder each have oval, round and angular bases, with age-specific feature anchors and elder wrinkles.
- Static portrait atlases use up to sixteen columns to avoid excessively tall textures. `row_lookup[variant_index][profile_index]` gives a physical cell index; cell coordinates are `(index % atlas_columns) * 48` and `(index // atlas_columns) * 48`. `sheet_rows` labels physical cells, including cells sharing pixel data across logical choices. Unused trailing atlas cells are transparent. No animation or simulation IDs change.
- Six genetic eye shapes and five fixed iris colours combine with nine eye poses. Four genetic brow shapes combine with seven poses. Nose has five shapes; mouth has the eleven shapes used by the expression table. `expressions.json` defines every one of the eleven emotion IDs through brows, eyes and mouth selections, independently of the genetic feature choices.
- Shaved is a null front/back hairstyle selection. The other nine styles select the corresponding front and optional back part, matching the world-sprite hairstyle names. Back hair is cut around the head, ears and neck/shoulder silhouette so it can occupy its declared late layer without covering the face or looking like a beard. Facial hair has four optional styles for adult/elder; children select no facial hair. Elder hair defaults to grey LUT row 6, with white row 7 also available.
- Missing-ear trauma is subtractive: clip the ear part with the profile-specific polygon in `scar_operations`, then draw the matching scar accent in the normal scars layer. Cheek and brow scars are small skin-ramp marks, without blood or gore. Cropped-hair scar previews keep the affected area visible.
- Portrait paint reuses the six world-sprite pattern IDs. Apply it only where the accumulated portrait's visible index belongs to the skin slot group, so it cannot cover eyes, hair or clothing. Leg marks are intentionally empty because legs fall outside the head-and-shoulders crop; arm and shoulder marks can be hidden by clothing. The paint preview uses bare shoulders to expose those patterns.
- The vignette uses two hard-edged culture-ramp bands. Native-size readability strips omit the vignette so grass/dark views and silhouettes show the person rather than the background disc. The 4x review views include the culture vignette. There are no partial-alpha gradients or baked shadows.
- Shoulders reuse all six clothing choices; the accessories match the world sprites, including the helmet's leather and bone material options. Iris colours reuse existing fixed palette slots, so no palette colours or slot indices are added. The portrait pivot `[24,46]` is a UI assembly anchor; world-foot pivots do not apply to bust portraits.
- Atlas addressing, layered composition, expression selection, scar subtraction and paint clipping are implemented in the review assembler and recorded in metadata. Game-side inspector/family-tree/chronicle integration remains outside this asset-only phase.

## Early structures — 2026-10-30

- Section 7.1 supplies eighteen complete-sprite IDs in this phase. `animal_pen`, `dock` and `bridge` explicitly use pieces, so they remain with the modular section 7.2 phase. Each complete sprite has one authored shape in each of the five required conditions, with the exact listed frame size. This produces 93 sheets and 102 frame images including the three lit strips.
- `asset_id` retains the specification ID. Filename and manifest suffixes distinguish conditions. Structural `state` is building, complete, damaged, ruined or burned; `lighting` is unlit or lit. The separate lit sheets select the complete condition only, rather than inventing a lighting-by-damage product not specified in section 7.1.
- Fire pit uses the required four lit frames. Kiln and smelter also use four because their animation count is unspecified and they share the same contained-fire convention. All three use 6 fps, chosen for readable flicker without rapid flashing. `emissive_slots` records the fixed fire ramp. Furnace animation is clipped to its original opening; smoke, glow and cast light remain separate effects.
- All sheets include index exports. Most building materials use their existing fixed ramp slots; living mound grass, raft lashings and totem carvings use the existing variable grass, bark and culture groups. No palette colours or slots are added. Materials are tagged without assigning a technological era.
- Pivots are bottom-centre of the footprint at `[width / 2, height - 2]`, with a transparent pixel below. Ground shadows are engine metadata only. Dark doorway, cavity and under-slab pixels describe the structure's form rather than a cast ground shadow.
- Construction silhouettes show exposed frames, partial masonry, stone piles or a stone resting on skids. Damage removes asymmetric contour chips and adds small material cracks. Ruins retain lower remnants and debris. Burned organic materials use basalt char; mineral surfaces use ash/soot colouring to show fire exposure, without asserting that stone itself burns in the simulation.
- Totem carvings are coloured grain accents, with a reserved 12x12 glyph anchor at `[2,10]`. They contain no generated symbol. Actual culture glyphs and their banned-shape rejection checks remain in section 9. Burial mound, cairn, standing stone and altar carry no fixed religious emblem; the cairn's cap is a flat stone rather than a cross-like peak.
- Contact sheets show all conditions, each lit frame, twelve totem culture recolours, and native grass/dark/silhouette views. The asset pipeline supplies these review composites; game-side structure placement, condition selection and effects integration remain outside this phase.

## Detailed civilisation direction — 2026-10-30

- The revised request expands the original close-view detail and civilisation scope. Keep the 16-unit logical grid, existing simulation IDs, original sprite sizes and prior files. Add a separate `civilisation` visual family with artwork authored at 64 px per tile and exported at densities 16, 32 and 64. This additive rule supersedes the original single-density assumption for this new family only; it does not silently resize earlier assets.
- The attachment establishes desired close-view readability and scale. Its artwork, branding, characters, interface and distinctive styling are not copied. All shipped art remains generated from original pixel rules, with the existing master palette, hard alpha, top-left light and no baked ground shadows.
- Density changes visual detail without changing world coordinates, footprints, item identity or storage contents. Close magnification has finite authored detail: 64 px per tile, with crisp integer enlargement beyond that. Planet-scale aggregation and actual camera transitions require engine integration. Trees distinguish their tall visual frame from their one-cell ground footprint.
- Open building layouts use separate floors, walls, doors, roofs and placed objects. Thirty-one specialist/home/camp layouts demonstrate equipment-led use. Roofs are removable per room, with a southern foreground-wall cutaway in close previews. Rectangular roof surfaces are a sample, not completion of section 7.2 ridge/gable/conical and irregular footprint sets. All twenty-three supplied wall materials have sixteen complete connection masks.
- Eight storage forms export separate backs, fronts and optional covers. Stored resources are separate sprites, support mixed contents, and use explicit depth-ordered anchors. Visible slots are display capacity only; the simulation remains responsible for actual mass, volume, stack limits, ownership, reservations and transport. Backpacks and carts expose attachment/transport metadata without implementing equipment or movement.
- A persistent-item example conserves six grain-stack identities and sixty grain while showing granary-to-cart-to-backpack-to-chest handoffs. These are review snapshots. They do not assert that runtime inventory transfers, theft, spoilage, save/load or logistics exist. No game or simulation code is changed.
- Materials and capabilities remain the runtime classification. Production-review groups span foraging to space technology without imposing an era progression. The coverage catalogue distinguishes sample art/layouts, art supplied by the landscape family and planned entries. It is a scope inventory, not a claim that every civilisation requirement has been drawn or implemented.
- The civilisation pack contains 671 visual components, 2,013 density sheets and 2,076 frame images. It includes a static scale reference, sixty-six nature/animal references and eleven vehicles. Wardrobe and landscape are separate additive families. Full detailed fauna motion, seasonal/growth flora silhouettes, building conditions, detailed overlays/effects and most advanced catalogue requirements remain pending. Existing low-resolution families are preserved as the usable base.
- Detailed active equipment uses four frames at 6 fps. Hearth/kiln/forge/oven also have unlit visuals. Furnace fire and lamp pixels expose emissive slots; glow, smoke, fuel, heat and cast light remain separate engine effects. The full scale/storage/composition contract is in `tools/assetgen/CIVILISATION.md`.

## Expanded wardrobe, regions and landscape — 2026-10-30

- The further variety request is implemented through additive `wardrobe` and `landscape` families and an expanded civilisation kit, all at 16/32/64 px density. Original files, IDs, frame counts and palette slots remain unchanged. The base logical tile remains sixteen units; higher density adds authored detail without changing world scale.
- Wardrobe has 185 part selections in twelve layers, 32 editable outfit presets and all twenty original action animations. It uses the shared people pose rig, supported age/build/direction profiles, original action IDs and original timing. Existing major layer order is preserved, with legwear, footwear, top, outerwear and armour inside the clothing group. Body paint is clipped to exposed skin after those parts.
- Main and trim dyes are independent per piece, each using one of thirteen existing cloth LUT rows. Skin, hair and culture choices remain separate. Natural metals retain their fixed material ramps; paintable parts, cloth and straps expose dye slots. Interpret the approximate sixteen-colour target per exported component: independently dyed layered outfits can contain additional colours after composition. No new master colours are required.
- Wardrobe stores whole animation strips in up to eight atlas columns. `row_lookup` is resolved separately at each density because small details can collapse into shared physical strips. Duplicate physical strips are removed. The shared hand anchors remain valid; held items rotate about their grip where needed to fit an action frame. Portrait atlases retain the prior clothing choices until their separate wardrobe synchronisation phase.
- Nine regional styles provide resource/climate preferences and 243 material/layout variants. They guide choices without locking architecture or clothing to ethnicity, nationality or fixed historical eras. Construction declarations list input categories and process requirements, not quantitative production recipes. Regional materials include wood/reed/bamboo, hides, earth/adobe, several stones, snow/ice, fired materials, metals and composites.
- Foods, minerals, fibres, hides and crafted-resource tokens are separate container-content art. Their logical 4x4 extent represents small stored stacks. They do not replace the specification's separate 16x16 UI icons and 8x8 world-drop sprites. Similar resources use recognisable forms where possible: grain heads, kernels, cobs, loaves, cheese wedges, fish, shellfish, jars, ore veins, ingots and bundles. There is no gore or baked text.
- Vehicle payloads are separate persistent containers, with attachment anchors in `vehicle_profiles.json`; they are not baked into ship or vehicle PNGs. A foreground hull polygon can redraw the base over the load for correct occlusion without exporting duplicate hull art. Empty/loaded transport reviews use the same container renderer as stationary storage.
- Political, royal, religious, ceremonial, rural, artisan, construction, industrial, defensive and magical layouts use equipment and capability tags. Ceremonial screens and magical foci have no alphabetic runes or real-world emblems. Actual procedural culture glyphs and their rejection validator remain in section 9.
- Landscape supplies 56 materials and eight cliff types across 384 sheets. Every material has four full-tile variants and all sixteen dual-grid masks at each density; tilled soil retains both orientations, and water/lava retain four frames. Corner geometry is recalculated on the same field at each density to preserve exact compatible edges. New material priority is anchored to the original material family, with a secondary offset that does not change any original ordering.
- Use random plain variants for homogeneous mask-15 terrain areas to reduce repetition. Surface borders remain seam-compatible. Exposed rock faces use internal form shading under top-left light; no ground shadows are baked. Dense grass and rock textures were reviewed and reduced where they made the repeating grid too obvious.
- Full game camera/detail switching, persistent inventory transfers, actual equipment selection, specialist jobs, navigation, production and transport remain outside the authorised art-only paths. The generators supply the art, metadata and review assemblers needed for those integration steps.
